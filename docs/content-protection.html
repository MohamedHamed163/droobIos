Droob — Content Protection

How the iOS app protects course videos from sharing, recording, and piracy — and what each layer buys you.

Defense in depth. No single trick stops piracy on a phone — so the app stacks independent layers. Each one closes a different gap; together they make casual sharing pointless and serious piracy expensive and traceable. FairPlay DRM is the only one that protects the video at the hardware level — it's the centerpiece (see below).

★ FairPlay DRM — the gold standard (most important)

Apple FairPlay Streaming (FPS) is the same DRM Netflix, Disney+ and Apple TV use. It is the only layer that protects the video itself at the OS/hardware level instead of reacting to events.

Why it's the strongest — the benefits:

  • Uncapturable video. Decryption happens in a secure media path. Screen recording, QuickTime-over-USB, and capture software receive a black frame for the video — not by us blanking it, but because the protected frames never leave the secure pipeline.
  • HDCP enforced automatically. Plug into a TV, monitor, projector, or HDMI capture card that isn't HDCP-compliant → the video goes black. This is hardware-level copy protection you can't code around.
  • The keys are entitlement-gated. The decryption key is only issued by your license server after confirming the user is enrolled in that course. A leaked manifest or stolen file is useless without a key — this is what actually stops re-distribution.
  • Per-user, per-asset, revocable. Keys can expire, be scoped per device, and be refused server-side — you stay in control after delivery.
  • Tamper-resistant. Unlike app-level tricks, DRM isn't defeated by a jailbreak tweak or a screen-recorder update.

Status: the iOS client handshake is fully built (AVContentKeySession). It needs the backend: an Apple FPS certificate, a license server (KSM), and FairPlay-encrypted HLS. Full runbook in FairPlay/SETUP.md.

Active on-device layers

Screen-capture black-out · UIScreen.isCaptured

Benefit: The whole app instantly blanks when the screen is recorded, mirrored (AirPlay), or captured by QuickTime/Xcode over a cable — and the lesson pauses so audio isn't recorded either.

How: iOS reports any "screen cloning" via isCaptured; we cover content + pause playback. A user toggle in Settings → Security can lock/unlock it app-wide.

Limit: Protects pixels + audio, but the (decrypted) video frame still technically exists in the buffer — FairPlay closes that.

Dynamic moving watermark

Benefit: The viewer's email/ID floats over the video at all times. If anyone films the screen with another camera (the one thing software can't block), the leak is permanently traceable to that account — a powerful deterrent.

How: A semi-transparent label drifts position every few seconds. Zero performance cost, always on, can't be turned off by the viewer.

Limit: Deters and traces; doesn't physically stop filming.

Viewer Presence Guard · front camera

Benefit: Enforces "one enrolled person, watching." 0 faces → pauses (possible off-camera rig); 2+ faces → "multiple viewers"; a phone in frame → recording alert.

How: Low-res front-camera frames analyzed a few times a second on a background thread (video never stutters), with debouncing so brief look-aways don't punish.

Limit: Needs camera consent; identity/liveness need the optional ML models.

Camera-required gate

Benefit: A protected lesson won't play without camera access — a viewer can't simply disable the presence guard to dodge monitoring.

How: If access is denied, the video is covered with an "Enable camera access" prompt (re-asks, or opens Settings).

Limit: Requires the explicit camera-consent flow (store-compliant).

External-display block

Benefit: Connecting a cable to a TV/monitor/capture card or mirroring → content blanks, blocking "play it on a big screen and record it."

How: Detects a second connected screen/scene and triggers the same black-out.

Limit: Full hardware enforcement on HDMI comes from FairPlay's HDCP.

iPad multitasking lockout

Benefit: The app refuses Split View, Slide Over, and the new iPadOS 26 windowing/tabs — so the lesson can't be parked beside a screen-recorder or notes app.

How: UIRequiresFullScreen + single-scene. OS-enforced; nothing to bypass.

Limit: iPad only (iPhone has no split anyway).

Jailbreak / integrity block

Benefit: On a jailbroken device — where capture protections can be stripped — the app blocks itself entirely.

How: Conservative checks (sandbox escape, jailbreak artifacts). Release builds only.

Limit: Detection is an arms race; raises the bar, not absolute.

Privacy side-dim

Benefit: Darkens the left/right edges to discourage shoulder-surfing — a visible "this is private" cue.

How: A gradient overlay focusing a central strip.

Limit: Cosmetic only — software can't change a screen's physical viewing angle (that needs a hardware privacy film).

What no iOS app can do (honest limits)

  • Stop a separate camera filming the screen. Physically impossible to block — countered by the watermark (makes the leak traceable) and the presence guard.
  • Change the screen's physical viewing angle in software — needs a hardware privacy film.
  • Detect a plain charging cable. There's no API — but it doesn't matter, because any actual capture over that cable is detected.
  • Run on / be installed on Android (Huawei) or as an APK. Different platform — an iOS app simply cannot; distribution is locked to the App Store / TestFlight per Apple ID.
Bottom line. The on-device layers (active today) stop casual recording and sharing and make every leak traceable. FairPlay (next step) adds the hardware-grade lock that makes the video itself uncapturable and the files worthless without an enrollment-gated key — turning "good deterrence" into "industry-standard protection."
